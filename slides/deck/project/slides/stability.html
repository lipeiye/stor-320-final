SPEAKER 4 · about 60 seconds We also stress-tested the two slopes that were left. With the classical 95 percent interval, both stay away from zero, so they look real. [CLICK] But the robust interval, which does not assume every county is equally noisy, is about three times wider, and both reach zero. And the bootstrap, which resamples the counties 2,000 times, gives a third answer. [CLICK] One reason is a single county. Robeson has by far the most pull on the fit. Without it, the transportation slope almost doubles. [CLICK] Also, neighboring counties have similar errors, so these intervals are probably too narrow. And when we redraw every estimate inside its CDC interval, all three social slopes include zero. So we cannot trust any single slope. Next, [Speaker 5] has two more tests.
