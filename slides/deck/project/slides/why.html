SPEAKER 4 · about 40 seconds The likely answer is in how the data are made. The CDC does not count COPD cases in every county. It starts from a national phone survey and builds a statistical model. For every measure, the model uses the same inputs: age, sex, race, education, and county poverty. So COPD, smoking, and our three social needs all come from one recipe. [CLICK] COPD against smoking is almost a perfect line, with a correlation of 0.99. But the grey lines show how uncertain each point really is. Source: CDC PLACES methodology (https://www.cdc.gov/places/methodology/index.html). The model is multilevel regression and poststratification on BRFSS answers, with age, sex, race/ethnicity and education from the survey, county poverty from the ACS, and state and county random effects. Correlations among the seven measures: 0.69 to 0.99.
