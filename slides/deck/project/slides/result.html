Once smoking is in the model, the three social indicators add almost nothing, and their slopes flip

Least-squares regression, 100 counties · leave-one-out error 0.177 to 0.165 points · bars: robust (HC3) 95% intervals

SPEAKER 3 · about 55 seconds Start on the left. Smoking alone already matches about 98 percent of the county differences in COPD. Adding all three social needs raises R-squared by only 0.007, and the prediction error drops only slightly. So they add almost nothing. On the right, the grey dots show the slope of each social need by itself. All three are clearly positive. [CLICK] The blue dots add smoking and inactivity. All three slopes drop to about zero. [CLICK] The orange dots put all five predictors in one model, and something strange happens. Transportation turns positive again, housing turns negative, and both intervals get very wide. We do not believe housing insecurity protects lungs. A flip like this is a warning sign: the model cannot tell these variables apart. Numbers: R-squared 0.977 (smoking alone), 0.978 (plus inactivity), 0.984 (plus three social indicators). Leave-one-out error 0.177 to 0.165 points. Slopes alone, with behaviors, all five: transport +0.38, -0.08, +0.21; housing +0.23, -0.08, -0.21; uninsured +0.46, +0.03, +0.02.
